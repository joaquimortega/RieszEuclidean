# Bochner construction and final theorem scope

Reviewed 2026-09-13 against `paper/RieszEuclidean.tex`, Section `sec:bochner`, Theorem `thm:triangle`, Remark `rem:general-boundary`, and Remark `rem:odd-polygons`. This was an independent source and mathematical-scope review, not an independent kernel, build, linter, or axiom-check run.

The construction supplies the precise representation-existence input used by the paper. I found no remaining spectral-existence hypothesis, sign mismatch, unjustified preservation of mass under vague convergence, or missing separability assumption in that invocation.

- [`BochnerExistence.lean`](../RieszEuclidean/BochnerExistence.lean), `exists_representsCorrelation_unitary`, treats strongly continuous unitary correlations on a complete separable complex Hilbert space. It does not claim the full Bochner theorem for every continuous positive-definite scalar function. This specialization is sufficient: `exists_hull_spectralMeasures` applies it to the actual hull Koopman operators, using `separableSpace_hullLp`, `strongContinuous_hullKoopmanUnitary`, and `hullKoopmanUnitary_add` from [`Koopman.lean`](../RieszEuclidean/Koopman.lean). The finite Borel measure conclusion is exactly `RepresentsCorrelation`; the scalar family is then chosen for all vectors. No measurable dependence on the vector is asserted or needed by the control-measure argument.
- The general unitary theorem does not separately assume `U 0 = id`. This is harmless: the group law and invertibility of each `U y` imply that identity. The concrete hull API proves it explicitly. The zero vector and the zero-dimensional Hilbert-space case are also allowed; the construction does not assume a nonzero basis vector.
- [`BochnerWindows.lean`](../RieszEuclidean/BochnerWindows.lean) uses the normalized window `1_C(x) U(-x) f / sqrt(|C|)`. [`PhysicalTranslationSpectrum.lean`](../RieszEuclidean/PhysicalTranslationSpectrum.lean) uses the spatial shift `g(x-y)` and the positive Fourier transform. Consequently `integral_inner_bochnerWindow_shift` and [`BochnerApproximateMeasures.lean`](../RieszEuclidean/BochnerApproximateMeasures.lean), `bochnerApproxMeasure_fourier`, give the positive-character transform `fejerWeight R y * unitaryCorrelation U f y`. Mathlib's `inner f (U y f)` is the paper's `⟨U_y f,f⟩` under its opposite linearity convention. The signs agree with `exp(2π i y·θ)` and the later cutoff `t + θ ∈ Ω`; no reflected spectrum is substituted.
- [`BochnerCoordinateWindows.lean`](../RieszEuclidean/BochnerCoordinateWindows.lean) justifies the countable coordinate summations through integrated Parseval and integrable square-norm bounds. The approximating measures are sums of actual nonnegative Fourier-density measures. Their total mass is exactly `‖f‖²`, proved in `bochnerApproxMeasure_univ` and `bochnerApproxMeasure_mass`. No prior abstract representing measure enters this step.
- [`BochnerMeasureLimit.lean`](../RieszEuclidean/BochnerMeasureLimit.lean), `exists_finiteMeasure_vague_limit`, obtains a finite positive measure through a weak-star cluster functional and Riesz–Markov on compact tests. Its refining filter is explicitly nontrivial; it need not be a subsequence. It proves only a mass upper bound at this stage, correctly allowing escape of mass.
- [`BochnerFourierLimit.lean`](../RieszEuclidean/BochnerFourierLimit.lean), `representsCorrelation_of_bounded_vague_limit`, resolves that issue. Uniform mass bounds allow dominated convergence against integrable Schwartz tests; their inverse Fourier transforms lie in `C₀`, where the vague limit is known. Equality of tested Fourier transforms gives equality almost everywhere, and continuity of the target correlation and the finite measure's transform upgrades this to equality everywhere. Evaluation at zero then recovers the full mass through [`SpectralMeasures.lean`](../RieszEuclidean/SpectralMeasures.lean), `RepresentsCorrelation.unitary_mass`. Tightness and preservation of total mass under vague convergence are not silently assumed. Uniqueness, the parallelogram/scaling identities, and the invariant constant's Dirac spectrum remain supplied by that existing module.

The main-result targets retain concrete domain semantics: [`Basic.lean`](../RieszEuclidean/Basic.lean), `HasExponentialRieszBasis`, asks for boundedly invertible synthesis with actual exponential columns in Lebesgue `L²` of the stated domain. The theorem `exists_hull_spectralMeasures` has exactly the universally quantified provider signature used by the conditional ball, triangle, ellipsoid, general-boundary, and polygon assemblies, so it discharges their `hrep` argument without changing their geometric hypotheses. The unconditional statements instantiate this provider.

For triangles, [`TriangleAffineAssembly.lean`](../RieszEuclidean/TriangleAffineAssembly.lean) uses actual positive barycentric coordinates and proves the affine normal form for every noncollinear vertex triple. For the general-boundary result, [`GeneralBoundaryAssembly.lean`](../RieszEuclidean/GeneralBoundaryAssembly.lean) retains the paper's finite nonzero supported boundary measure, null translated overlaps, null ambient frontier, and almost-everywhere positive-volume separation in every neighborhood. It adds no convexity, smoothness, or local halfspace requirement.

The polygon conclusion uses the interior of a bounded finite irredundant supporting-halfspace polygon. Nonzero normals and a nonempty strict face for every index encode its genuine sides. [`PolygonPresentation.lean`](../RieszEuclidean/PolygonPresentation.lean) proves, rather than assumes, their equivalence with literal maximal nondegenerate boundary segments: `supportingFaceEquivMaximalBoundarySegments`, `maximalBoundarySegments_card`, `strictSupportingFace_eq_openSegment`, and `closedSupportingFaces_parallel_iff`. Normalization is proved to preserve the domain and faces. [`PolygonParity.lean`](../RieszEuclidean/PolygonParity.lean) derives an unpaired direction from odd cardinality. Thus neither the parity conclusion nor the side count is hidden in an abstract geometry hypothesis.

One scope qualification should remain explicit in documentation: the polygon input is this supporting-halfspace presentation. The reviewed files do not construct such a presentation from a separate vertex-list or convex-hull polygon type. This is a conventional concrete encoding of convex polygons, with geometric side semantics proved internally, but it is not a claimed transport theorem from every other polygon representation. The results concern open interiors, as in the manuscript's polygon remark.
